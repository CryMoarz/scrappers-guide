# What's new

Plain notes for people using the guide. The app shows the latest few under "What's new" on its front page. Details for developers are in the commits.

## 4.6.0 (19 November 2026)

- Water through a Canadian winter: melting snow and ice (old snow and ice give far more water per pot), keeping water from freezing, getting water through lake ice safely, and what boiling a litre costs in wood, gas, alcohol or electricity.
- The house: draining a house you can't heat without losing its water, which water in a building you can drink and which you never can (heating loops, antifreeze, pools, hot tubs, waterbeds), and what to do when sewage backs up into the basement because the town's pumps stopped.
- Finding and treating water: what each kind of surface water tends to carry, testing water with found pool, aquarium and lead kits, testing how strong old bleach still is, clearing muddy water with alum, a biosand filter, a stovetop still, an RO unit on 12 or 24 V, chlorine from salt for experienced people, and checking a filter that may have frozen.
- Wells: a bucket for a drilled well with no power, shock-chlorinating a well or cistern, driving a sandpoint well, a rope pump, and repairing hand pumps, jet pumps and cracked containers. Hauling water by cart, sled or roller.
- Toilets: toilets when the ground is frozen, a compost toilet, a ventilated pit latrine and a tippy-tap hand-washing station. New jobs for finding water, dealing with human waste and keeping clean, and the water refill machines outside supermarkets as a new device.
- Safer water advice in older cards: pool and hot-tub water is for washing and flushing only, never drinking; water from an RO unit still needs disinfecting; cold or cloudy water needs double the bleach and 1 to 2 hours; chlorine doesn't stop Cryptosporidium, so boil surface water; one free-chlorine target of 0.5 mg/L after treating; and solar disinfection doesn't work in a Canadian winter.
- The water heater teardown no longer has you undo a gas pipe, and the biohazard card's bleach mix is 1 part to 9 of water.
- Quick reference has Water and toilets and Heat and winter groups, so the winter cards are easy to find.
- Over 700 corrections from checking every new card against its sources.

## 4.5.0 (19 November 2026)

- Staying warm through a Canadian winter: heating one room, hypothermia in a cold house, condensation and mould, warm clothes and where nobody else looks for them, an RV or mobile home through the winter, and a solar air heater for a south window.
- Wood heat from start to finish: checking and fixing a found wood stove, installing it with firebrick and chimney pipe, checking and sweeping a chimney, heating with an open fireplace, what to burn and what never to, cooking on a wood stove, heating water with a stove coil, and storing heat for the night in water, brick and masonry. Also a barrel stove from a steel drum, and hot work on drums, tanks and cylinders before you cut one.
- Firewood: getting a winter's worth in town, telling dry wood from wet without a meter, splitting safely, clearing fallen trees (spring poles, hung trees and power lines), chainsaw chain filing and upkeep, bow saws, axe care and re-handling, and checking a found hammer or axe. Chainsaw chaps are a new kind of protective gear.
- Fuel heat and light: kerosene heaters, diesel air heaters, construction heaters, portable propane heaters, gas fireplaces, camp stoves, lanterns, wick lamps and candles. Reading a heater's label, gas at a damaged house, gas appliances that work with no power, running a furnace from a generator, cooking indoors in an outage, and keeping lamp oil away from children.
- Every device that burns fuel now says whether it may burn indoors (outdoors only, vented, or rated for unvented indoor use), what it burns, how much heat it gives and whether it needs power. 14 new devices, among them wood and pellet stoves, boilers, oil furnaces, heat recovery ventilators and log splitters, and new part types for candles, lighters, hand warmers, space blankets, firebrick and chimney pipe.
- Detectors and meters: 15 kinds, from CO and smoke alarms to gas monitors, Geiger counters, radon kits, voltage testers, IR thermometers, thermal cameras and water test kits. Each says what it can and can't tell you, how to prove a found one works, and where they're kept.
- 22 new hazard cards: cold, thin ice, wildlife, stinging insects, confined spaces, grain bins, sour gas, radon, silica dust, lime and lye, blue-green algae, machinery that catches clothing, lines that snap, flying fragments, falling ice, molten metal, open flames, self-heating materials, heating sealed containers, treated seed, ammonia refrigeration plants, and found ammunition, fireworks and flares. Older cards gained sourced advice on raccoon latrines and bat droppings, dating old paint, where asbestos hides, and pet poisons.
- Over 750 corrections from checking every new card against its sources, and older cards brought into line: a CO alarm's test button checks the electronics, not the sensor; propane falters in deep cold; forklift tanks feed liquid and can flood a burner; foam insulation burns fast with toxic smoke; red kidney beans must boil hard before slow cooking; garden hose can hold lead.
- Guide steps can link to other guides, in the full app and in simple mode.
- The map sorts buildings more carefully: an industrial building is a factory and an industrial area is warehouses, ice rinks are arenas, tall residential buildings are apartments, and shelters and food banks no longer show as nursing homes.

## 4.4.0 (18 November 2026)

- Protective gear: 25 kinds, from dust masks and respirators to safety glasses, gloves, earplugs, hard hats, boots, coveralls and fall harnesses. Each says what it protects against and what it doesn't, how to read its markings and spot fakes, how to tell a found one is still good, what to use without one, and which places keep it on hand and where inside.
- Search finds gear kept on shelves, not just parts inside devices: "hard hat" points you to construction sites, hardware stores and water plants. Building pages list the supplies kept there, and hazard cards link to the gear that helps.
- 22 hazard cards give better protection advice, checked against safety bodies: boots are not electrical protection, arc flash needs an arc-rated shield, asbestos needs a half mask with P100 filters rather than a disposable, thin nitrile gloves are splash protection only, and a fall harness needs a strong anchor, enough room below and never working alone. A new card covers loud noise.
- 20 new teardown guides: hoverboards, robot and stick vacuums, electric toothbrushes, battery yard tools, riding mowers, pressure washers, gas grills, window and central air conditioners, chest freezers, air purifiers, blenders, humidifiers, projectors, holiday decorations, bicycles, two-way radios, car USB chargers and mattresses. Teardowns now cover 97 of 181 kinds of device. The gas grill, central AC and projector guides are marked for experienced people only.
- Nearly 100 fixes to device data found while writing them: missing hazards and parts, a hoverboard pack is 20 cells, not 10, and two unsafe notes corrected (never short an AC capacitor with a screwdriver, and never move a grill's regulator to another gas appliance).
- The refrigerant safety guide now says crimping lines shut works only on tubing your pinch-off tool fits; leave a central air conditioner's loop sealed.

## 4.3.0 (17 November 2026)

- 20 new teardown guides: smartphones, tablets, power banks, phone chargers, flat TVs, Wi-Fi routers, emergency lights, garage door openers, mini fridges, clothes dryers, gas furnaces, range hoods, toaster ovens, space heaters, coffee makers, hair dryers, ceiling fans, corded power tools, petrol mowers and vacuums. Each is written from cited sources and fact-checked against every one, with the safety steps first.
- Search understands more of how people ask: plurals and "12 volt", typing slips like "batery", a part with a device ("car battery" or "laptop battery" puts that device first), names like "air conditioner", and needs like "heat my house", "shower" or "filter smoke".
- Searching for a hazard ("asbestos", "capacitor discharge", "lead acid") shows its safety card first.
- On the map, daycares and kindergartens are their own kind of building instead of counting as schools.
- Mains-powered appliances now carry the mains electricity hazard and hot ones the burn hazard, with more device fixes found while writing the teardowns (a furnace's igniter, a mower's blade, a vacuum's filter).
- The refrigerant safety guide and the compressor teardown now agree: never cut an intact loop open; crimp both lines first.

## 4.2.0 (17 November 2026)

- A website: every device, part type, guide, pinout and hazard as a page you can read or link to, with the app to open or download, at crymoarz.github.io/scrappers-guide.
- Security fixes from a full review. For moderators: the Review tab now works out each change on your own device and checks its signature, and your review counts only for the exact change you saw, so nobody between you and the hub can slip a different one through. Update the app (or tools/mod.js) to review on a 4.2.0 hub.
- Field notes only move forward: an old copy of a note, sent again by anyone, can no longer undo its author's update.
- A comment a moderator hides now disappears from nodes and phones that already had it.
- The app is stricter with what it receives: a file can't roll its data back, an update is offered only for a newer version, a damaged or bad database can't lock it (it falls back to its built-in data), and a hub's word alone can't make your unsent edits disappear.
- Hubs and nodes stay up when attacked: a broken or hostile offline map, a hostile upstream hub, slow or flooding clients and costly searches no longer hang or crash them, and place searches in the far north read far fewer map tiles. A web page can no longer reach a hub's peers-only notes or AI tools through your browser.
- LoRa: field notes sent with ?seen are limited per hour and per area, marked as unverified radio reports, and never passed on over the air or to NOMAD's map. One radio can't spoil another's message.
- The Docker image ships the signed app, so apps that sync with it are offered updates again, and the services and container run with fewer rights.
- Faster searches for models and for what you can build.

## 4.1.0 (17 November 2026)

- Solar sizing for anywhere in the world, offline: type a place or use your location, and the solar calculator suggests a winter tilt, shows the sun on the panel month by month from NASA's 25-year averages built into the app, and sizes panel, battery and controller for your worst month or for summer only. It warns about freezing nights for lithium batteries and says when a month has too little sun for solar alone.
- A LoRa airtime and range calculator: time on air for every Meshtastic preset, how many messages a minute the channel carries once nodes rebroadcast, the link budget, and the radio horizon from both antenna heights.
- Run and grow a town LoRa mesh: airtime per preset, how fast the channel fills, what the firmware holds back as the air gets busy, how messages cross the mesh, finding a failing relay and adding a neighbourhood.
- Hand over a town LoRa mesh, for when the builder is gone: a logbook and map, a card for every relay, backing up settings and keys safely, loaded spares, seasonal power checks and an offline kit.
- The town mesh guide covers realistic range, relays that overlap without crowding, and winter sun in Canada. Fixed: device telemetry is off by default in current firmware, not sent every 30 minutes.
- The node guide's offline kit now builds the command-line tools with pip wheel, so they install with no internet.

## 4.0.0 (16 November 2026)

- First public release. The code is under the MIT License and the data under CC BY-SA 4.0.
- Board pinouts: 26 boards drawn pin by pin, 13 popular ones and 13 Chinese clones and knock-offs, with the boot, strapping, input-only and power pins marked and every header order checked against its maker's documents.
- Microcontrollers cheat sheet: which board you have, what voltage its pins take, which USB chip and driver it needs, how to force each family into its bootloader, the faults that stop boards booting, clone boards, and reflashing ESP smart plugs and bulbs with Tasmota or ESPHome.
- Microcontroller code templates in Arduino C++ and MicroPython: blink and serial, a debounced button, battery voltage, a low-voltage disconnect, a thermostat relay that fails safe, PWM through a MOSFET, an I2C scanner and ESP32 deep sleep.
- LoRa frequencies by country: the band, power limit and duty cycle in 211 countries and territories, with the matching Meshtastic region.
- Two new guides: build a LoRa mesh node from parts, and a LoRa mesh that carries text messages across a whole town with no phone network, internet or grid.
- On a NOMAD box, sources on sites that an installed Kiwix library copies get an offline link to that copy.
- Six more libraries linked: the Arduino, Raspberry Pi and Robotics Q&A sites, the OpenWrt wiki, and the C++ and Python references.
- Shop chemicals: making lye from wood ash.
- Fixed: the run and shopping list buttons on Plan, such as Delete this run, did nothing after visiting the map. The battery card now gives 12.2 V as half charge, matching its own table.

## 3.10.0 (12 November 2026)

- Quick reference: warning labels and symbols drawn out (chemical pictograms, machine warning triangles, the diamonds on trucks and tanks, the fire diamond, electrical markings, IP ratings, laser classes, plastic codes), plus cards for component codes, wire colours, connectors and pinouts, what fits what, water filters, date codes and era rules, power draw and battery runtime, and units. - Measured weights for ten electronics devices, so the carry limit can judge them.
- About says what the project is, with a legend of what the marks mean and a plain disclaimer.
- Fixed: part numbers are no longer read as ratings, and searching a chip number finds the models that use it. “Sync now” during an automatic sync now sends what you just added.
